# Ethics checklist

The current phase uses only public, non-identifiable data. Items marked **(people)** apply if the project later surveys or interviews renters, students or workers. An ethics board, not the researcher, decides whether a study counts as human-subjects research.

## 1. Informed consent (people)

- [ ] Consent form in plain language a non-expert can follow
- [ ] States who the researcher is (a high school student) and names the adult supervisor
- [ ] Explains the purpose, what participants will do and how long it takes
- [ ] Says participation is voluntary: people can skip questions or stop at any time with no penalty
- [ ] Explains what data is collected, where it is stored, who sees it and when it is deleted
- [ ] States risks and benefits honestly, with no overpromising
- [ ] Gives contact information for the researcher, supervisor and ethics board
- [ ] Screens out people under 18, or has a plan for parental permission plus the minor's agreement
- [ ] Online surveys show a consent page with an "I agree" button before any questions
- [ ] Form available in other languages if needed (many newcomers are international)
- [ ] Any gift card is small enough not to pressure anyone, and is kept even if a participant withdraws
- [ ] Separate opt-ins for audio recording and for being quoted

## 2. Confidentiality and data protection

**Public data (current phase)**

- [x] City assessor data has owner names and mailing addresses: not used in this analysis
- [x] Results reported citywide or by metro area, never by address or owner
- [x] Official downloads and published figures used instead of scraping listing sites
- [x] No names, phone numbers, emails or photos from listings stored
- [x] Every source logged with the date accessed (see [data/README.md](../data/README.md))

**Participant data (people)**

- [ ] Collect only what is needed (a rent range and ZIP code, not an exact address)
- [ ] Replace names with ID codes and keep the key in a separate, locked file
- [ ] Store data in a password-protected account with two-factor authentication
- [ ] Never commit raw participant data to this or any public repository, or paste it into AI tools
- [ ] Suppress any group smaller than about 5 people in reported results
- [ ] Delete recordings after transcription and set a deletion date for raw data

## 3. Potential harm

- [ ] **Emotional:** rent, debt, eviction and roommate conflict are stressful topics. Let people skip questions and keep a list of housing resources
- [ ] **Legal or housing risk:** avoid recording identifiable answers about subletting, unofficial units or lease violations
- [ ] **Immigration-related risk:** do not ask about visa or immigration status
- [ ] **Financial privacy:** ask for income and rent in ranges
- [x] **Harm from findings:** report patterns, never name "overpriced" buildings or landlords
- [x] **Misleading users:** show ranges and data dates so no one relies on a single number
- [ ] **Community impact:** consider how results could be used, for example to justify rent increases
- [ ] **Disclosures:** plan in advance what to do if someone mentions homelessness or unsafe housing

## 4. Power dynamics

- [ ] Do not recruit people who depend on the researcher (students tutored, club members) without an easy, private way to say no
- [ ] Never share tenant responses with landlords or property managers
- [ ] Make clear the project is independent of Harvard and MIT
- [ ] Recruiters (professors, employers, landlords) never learn who took part
- [ ] As a minor interviewing adults: supervisor reviews scripts, meetings happen in public places or online
- [ ] Do not give legal advice; point newcomers to official tenant resources
- [x] State whose voices the data leaves out (subsidized housing, informal and roommate arrangements)

## 5. Researcher positionality

- [x] Positionality statement written ([positionality.md](positionality.md))
- [x] Household tenure (renter) disclosed
- [x] Starting assumption ("rents keep rising") stated and tested, not just confirmed
- [ ] Check interpretations with newcomers
- [ ] Disclose any conflicts of interest and who supervises the project
- [ ] Use neutral survey wording ("How would you describe your rent?")
- [ ] Revisit the statement after data collection

## 6. IRB / ethics board submission (needed only for the people phase)

- [ ] Get a written ruling on whether review is needed
- [ ] Find a board: school science fair review committee, a research program's IRB, a mentor's university IRB, or a paid independent IRB
- [ ] Adult supervisor or mentor agrees to sign off
- [ ] Complete human-subjects training (for example, the CITI Program course) and save the certificate
- [ ] Prepare the packet: protocol summary, recruitment materials, consent forms, survey or interview questions, data management plan, risk/benefit statement, conflict-of-interest disclosure
- [ ] Ask which review level fits: exempt, expedited or full board
- [ ] No recruitment or data collection from people before written approval
- [ ] Submit an amendment before any change in questions, population or data sources
- [ ] Report problems (a data leak, a participant complaint) to the board right away
- [ ] For competitions (science fairs, Regeneron ISEF or STS), check their human-participant rules early
