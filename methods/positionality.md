# Researcher positionality statement

I moved to Cambridge, Massachusetts, from California for family reasons, and my household rents its home here. I have searched for apartments in this market myself, so I share some experiences with the newcomers this study aims to serve, such as unfamiliar neighborhoods and high, hard-to-compare prices. This insider view helped me recognize what information is hard to find, but it may also lead me to assume other newcomers share my priorities. Because I moved for family rather than for school or a job, my experience may differ from that of students and workers. Coming from California may also shape what I consider "expensive." As a renter, I have a personal stake in rising costs, which may make me more inclined to frame high rents as a problem.

I began this project believing that Cambridge rents have risen steadily. That belief shaped my research question and my focus on identifying "reliable" prices, and it may have made me more attentive to evidence of rising costs than to evidence against it. The city's own inflation-adjusted data, which show the median one-bedroom rent below its 2014 peak, challenged this assumption. My deductive codebook also reflects my interest in data reliability, so it emphasizes provenance and accuracy over other qualities a source might offer.

I relied on publicly available government data and used AI tools to help collect and organize it, which may favor official, easily accessible sources. To limit these influences, I document my coding decisions, compare sources against one another, and plan to have a second coder review a subset of the data.
